# Purpose

This specification defines how Atlas Resources establish, maintain, and communicate relationships with one another.

Relationships transform individual Resources into connected engineering knowledge.

They provide the structural foundation for engineering reasoning, navigation, validation, interoperability, and intelligent analysis.

---

# Scope

This specification defines:

- Resource Relationships
- Relationship Types
- Relationship Characteristics
- Relationship Rules
- Relationship Responsibilities

This specification does not define:

- Resource Identity
- Resource Classification
- Resource Properties
- Resource Semantics

---

# Definition

A **Resource Relationship** is an explicit connection between two or more Atlas Resources.

Relationships provide engineering context by describing how Resources interact, depend upon, or relate to one another.

Resources gain meaning not only from their individual characteristics but also from the network of relationships they participate in.

---

# Design Goals

The Resource Relationship Model is designed to provide:

- Engineering context
- Graph-based navigation
- AI reasoning
- Dependency tracking
- Interoperability
- Traceability
- Explainability
- Extensibility

---

# Relationship Principles

## Principle 1 — Resources Never Exist in Isolation

Every Atlas Resource may participate in one or more relationships.

Relationships provide engineering context.

---

## Principle 2 — Relationships Are Explicit

Relationships must be intentionally defined.

Atlas should never assume engineering relationships without evidence or user confirmation.

---

## Principle 3 — Relationships Have Meaning

Every relationship represents a specific engineering concept.

Relationships should never be generic.

---

## Principle 4 — Relationships Are Independent

Changing a Resource's properties does not automatically change its relationships.

Relationships evolve independently.

---

## Principle 5 — Relationships Enable Intelligence

Engineering intelligence emerges from connected Resources rather than isolated data.

The richer the relationship graph, the deeper Atlas can reason.

---

# Relationship Model

Every relationship consists of:

```
Source Resource

↓

Relationship Type

↓

Target Resource
```

Example

```
Wall

↓

Contains

↓

Door
```

---

# Relationship Characteristics

Every relationship should define:

- Source Resource
- Target Resource
- Relationship Type
- Direction
- Strength (optional)
- Metadata (optional)
- Status
- Created Date

---

# Relationship Types

Atlas supports multiple relationship categories.

---

## Structural Relationships

Describe physical or logical structure.

Examples:

- Contains
- Part Of
- Parent Of
- Child Of
- Composed Of

---

## Spatial Relationships

Describe spatial context.

Examples:

- Adjacent To
- Above
- Below
- Inside
- Outside
- Intersects
- Connected To

---

## Functional Relationships

Describe engineering purpose.

Examples:

- Supports
- Controls
- Supplies
- Depends On
- Protects
- Serves

---

## Reference Relationships

Describe informational links.

Examples:

- References
- Documents
- Specifies
- Approves
- Reviews

---

## Workflow Relationships

Describe engineering processes.

Examples:

- Assigned To
- Produces
- Requires
- Validates
- Approves

---

## AI Relationships

Describe intelligent interactions.

Examples:

- Suggested By
- Generated By
- Reviewed By
- Verified By

---

# Relationship Direction

Relationships may be:

## Directed

```
Room

Contains

Wall
```

Direction matters.

---

## Bidirectional

```
Room

Adjacent To

Corridor
```

Both Resources recognize the relationship.

---

# Relationship Cardinality

Atlas supports:

```
1 → 1

1 → Many

Many → 1

Many → Many
```

The relationship model should not impose unnecessary restrictions.

---

# Relationship Integrity

Relationships must always reference valid Atlas Resources.

Broken references should never exist.

Deleting a Resource must not silently invalidate the engineering graph.

Relationship integrity must always be preserved.

---

# Relationship Graph

Atlas represents engineering knowledge as a graph.

```
Building
│
├── Floor
│     │
│     ├── Room
│     │      │
│     │      ├── Wall
│     │      │      ├── Door
│     │      │      └── Window
│     │      │
│     │      └── Furniture
│     │
│     └── Corridor
│
└── Site
```

The graph is the foundation for navigation, reasoning, and analysis.

---

# Responsibilities

Resource Relationships are responsible for:

- Connecting Resources
- Preserving engineering context
- Supporting navigation
- Enabling graph traversal
- Supporting AI reasoning
- Supporting validation
- Supporting impact analysis

Relationships are not responsible for:

- Identity
- Classification
- Property values
- Lifecycle
